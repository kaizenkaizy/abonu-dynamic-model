1. Introduction & Core Concept
When analyzing human relationships, many people assume that connections build up like simple addition (A + B = C). However, through real-world observation, I noticed a major flaw in treating relationships like basic addition: spending extra time with someone cannot make up for a total lack of trust or mutual respect.
To fix this flaw, I created The Abonu Dynamic Model of Connection. It is a non-linear mathematical framework designed to calculate the real strength, sensitivity, and stability of a relationship.
2. Mathematical Definition
The overall Connection Strength (C) between two people is calculated using the product of three core variables:
                                                     C = T x E x R

Each variable is measured on a normalized decimal scale from 0.0 (completely absent) to 1.0 (maximum level):
1.	T — Trust Factor (0.0 < or = T < or =1.0): Measures honesty, emotional safety, reliability, and truthfulness										
2.	E — Shared Experience Metric (0.0 <or E < or = 1.0): Measures quality time spent together, overcoming common challenges, and shared memories.					
3.	R — Mutual Respect Coefficient (0.0 < or = R < or = 1.0): Measures how much both people respect each other’s personal goals, boundaries, character, and space.
Since all three inputs are decimals between 0.0 and 1.0, the output C also results in a decimal score between 0.0 and 1.0.
3. Fundamental Axioms & System Rules
Axiom 1: Abonu's Zero Property (The Nullifying Rule)
If any single input drops to zero, the entire connection score instantly drops to zero:
                    If T = 0  or  E = 0  or  R = 0  or  C = 0

•	Real-World Meaning: No matter how much time two people spend together (E = 1.0) or how much respect exists (R = 1.0), if trust is completely gone (T = 0.0), a genuine connection cannot exist (C = 0.0).								
Axiom 2: Fractional Dampening Rule
Because the variables are decimals between 0 and 1, a very low score in one area heavily pulls down the final result, even if the other two scores are high.
•	Sample Calculation:
o	High Shared Experience (E = 0.9)
o	High Mutual Respect (R = 0.9)
o	Low/Uncertain Trust (T = 0.2)
                                     C = 0.2 x 0.9 x 0.9 = 0.162

Even with a lot of history and high respect, severe doubt in trust pulls the total relationship score down to 0.162																																																																																																																																																																																																																					
4. Percentage Conversion & High-Sensitivity Scale
To convert the decimal output C into a readable percentage (C%), multiply by 100:
				C% = (T x E x R) x 100%

To give the model high diagnostic sensitivity, the percentage is broken down into 20 exact 5% sensitivity bands:
Range (C%)
Sensitivity Level	Diagnostic & Structural State
0.0% - 4.9%	Level 0: Total Collapse	Complete system failure. Trust, experience, or respect is at zero.
5.0% - 9.9%	Level 1: Critical Friction	Bare trace of contact. High hostility or pure surface fake behavior.
10.0% - 14.9%	Level 2: Transactional	Interacting purely out of necessity or forced proximity. No emotional care.
15.0% - 19.9%	Level 3: Peripheral	Distant recognition. Aware of each other, but trust is unverified.
20.0% - 24.9%	Level 4: Initial Acquaintance	Basic politeness established. Foundation exists, but zero stress tolerance.
25.0% - 29.9%	Level 5: Casual Contact	Regular surface chats. Good in low-stakes moments, but deep trust is absent.
30.0% - 34.9%	Level 6: Early Alignment	Respect and trust verified in small matters, though time together is short.
35.0% - 39.9%	Level 7: Functional Support	Reliable for daily tasks, routine school work, and minor projects.
40.0% - 44.9%	Level 8: Moderate Bond	Solid baseline. Boundaries are consistently honored by both sides.
45.0% - 49.9%	Level 9: Balanced Partnership	Steady daily interaction. Trust holds up fine through minor disagreements.
50.0% - 54.9%	Level 10: Tipping Point Threshold	The Stability Pivot. The relationship moves into genuine emotional safety.
55.0% - 59.9%	Level 11: Resilient Connection	Clear alignment. Both people actively protect the relationship and individual goals.
60.0% - 64.9%	Level 12: High Trust Baseline	High transparency. Low risk of misunderstanding or hidden motives.

90.0% - 94.9%	Level 18: Peak Synergistic Bond	Full mutual trust, rich shared history, and complete backing for personal goals.
95.0% - 100.0%	Level 19: Theoretical Optimal	Maximum theoretical limit (T = 1.0, E = 1.0, R = 1.0). Absolute alignment.
																																																																			
5.	Applied Test Case
•	Calculated Values: T = 0.85, E = 0.40, R = 0.90

•	Math Step:
C = 0.85 x 0.40 x 0.90 = 0.306
C% = 0.306 x 100% = 30.6%
•	Model Diagnostic: Level 6: Early Alignment (30.0% - 34.9%)
•	Analysis: The bond has strong trust (85%) and high respect (90%). Because time together is still low (40%), the score lands at Level 6. It is a healthy, safe relationship that will naturally climb to higher levels as more shared experiences accumulate over time.																																																																		
6. Limitations
1.	Subjective Rating: Scoring trust or respect as exact numbers (0.0 - 1.0) depends on honest personal observation rather than an automated machine reading.
2.	Time Sensitivity: The formula gives a reading for one exact moment. Future work on this model will focus on using probability tracking over weeks and months to watch how scores move dynamically.
